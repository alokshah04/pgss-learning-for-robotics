PGSS Project

Learning for Robotics

A hands-on introduction to the machine learning techniques behind modern robot control.

About

What do autonomous vehicles, ChatGPT, and superhuman chess bots have in common? Each deploys deep neural networks to make predictions or decisions across a sequence of steps. Robotics is especially rich with difficult problems, and hence, serves as an ideal testbed for pushing the frontier of machine learning. This project will rigorously study learning algorithms for robotics from theory to practice and will culminate in a project tackling an open problem of the team’s choosing. We will begin with a primer on the requisite mathematical tools, namely elementary selections from statistics, calculus, and linear algebra. Subsequently, we will introduce the supervised learning paradigm and popular learning algorithms within it, including linear models and neural networks with backpropagation, after which students will be equipped to train their first robot policy from expert demonstrations. We will then continue to reinforcement learning, where students will train a robot that learns from only its own interactions with the environment. Finally, students will familiarize themselves with and select an advanced topic to work on, including but not limited to Q-learning, continual or lifelong learning, model-based approaches, and vision-language-action (VLA) models.

Soft Prerequisites

Probability & Expectation Basic Calculus Linear Algebra

No prior robotics or ML experience required. The prerequisites are soft for a reason — all necessary background will be provided. We only ask for curiosity and a willingness to learn, which, of course, you already have since you were selected for PGSS in the first place ;)

Materials

A laptop with internet access. Google Colab free tier handles all compute — no local GPU needed. Colab Pro, which your Andrew ID gives you access to, may help for longer runs.

A free Overleaf account for writing the final paper in LaTeX. You already have access through your Andrew ID.

All starter code and theory exercises (ungraded, solely for understanding) will be self-contained — no prior setup required.

Schedule

Week 1

Foundations

  • Brief history of robotics and where learning based approaches enter the picture
  • Math review: statistics, calculus, linear algebra
  • ML primer: supervised learning, gradient descent, neural networks, and backpropagation
  • Hands on: Use your knowledge to train a simple robot to walk using Imitation Learning (IL)
›
Week 2

Reinforcement Learning

  • Markov Decision Processes, Policy Gradient Theorem, and the REINFORCE estimator
  • Advantage Estimation, Trust Region and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) along with popular variants
  • Hands-on: train a model to walk using PPO in MuJoCo
  • Results form the baseline for your paper
›
Week 3

Advanced Topics

  • Potential topics: Q-Learning, Continual Learning, Model-Based Approaches, VLAs, and whatever else you find interesting
  • Assigned readings; group discussion to pick a focus area
  • Begin collaborative research and write-up
›
Weeks 4–5

Project & Paper

  • Continue experiments on the chosen advanced topic
  • Iterate on the research write-up
  • Final presentation of results
